BingeJam lets friends watch Netflix, JioHotstar or Prime Video together ("watch parties") and listen to Spotify, YouTube Music, Amazon Music or YouTube together ("jams"), each on their own Android phone, by keeping play, pause and position in sync. Friends can also chat and, if they choose, see what each other is playing. This policy explains what the app reads, what it stores, why, and your rights. It is written to meet India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 ("DPDP Act") and the Information Technology Act, 2000 and its rules. BingeJam is not affiliated with Netflix, JioHotstar, Amazon, Spotify or YouTube.

Who is responsible

BingeJam is developed and operated by Aritraditya Roy, India (the "Data Fiduciary" under the DPDP Act). Contact: aritraditya.roy@gmail.com.

Your account

  • You sign in with Google or with a one-time sign-in link sent to your email. We receive your email address, and from Google also your name and profile photo address.
  • Your email address is private: it's stored only for your own account and is never shown to other users.
  • You choose a unique username, a display name and optionally a short bio and a profile picture (a photo or GIF you pick). These form your public profile, which other signed-in BingeJam users can see and find by username. If you sign in with Google, your Google profile photo may be used until you change or remove it.

What BingeJam reads on your phone

  • Media sessions of supported apps (Netflix, JioHotstar, Prime Video, Spotify, YouTube Music, Amazon Music, YouTube): whether something is playing or paused, the position, which controls the app supports, and the title, subtitle, length and artwork the app publishes for Android's media controls. In a watch party or jam, BingeJam sends the app play, pause and seek commands, and in a jam asks your music or YouTube app to start the track the host is playing (by its public Spotify or YouTube link, or by searching its title). Android only allows this for apps with Notification Access, which is why BingeJam asks for it.
  • BingeJam never reads your notifications, messages or their content, and never records or transmits your screen, video or audio.
  • Clipboard, once: on its very first launch the app checks the clipboard one time for a BingeJam invite link (the invite page puts it there so a fresh install can open your friend's room). Anything else is ignored; nothing from it is stored or sent. Android shows its own notice when this happens.

Activity sharing (you choose)

  • Sharing is off for every app until you turn it on, app by app (for example Netflix on, Spotify off). You can change this or go invisible at any time in Settings.
  • For the apps you share, your friends only see what you're playing: the app, title, subtitle, a small artwork thumbnail, whether it's playing, and whether you're in a watch party or jam and with whom (the display names of the others in that room, which any signed-in user can already see). They also see whether you're online and when you were last seen. It's cleared when you stop playing, go invisible or go offline.

What is stored, and where

Profile pictures you upload are stored on Cloudflare (Workers KV) and served from a public link that only appears on your profile; removing your picture deletes it. Everything else is stored in Google Firebase (Realtime Database, Singapore region), so your data is processed outside India by these providers:

  • your profile (username, display name, photo address, bio) and private account record (email, sign-in method, date of birth). Your date of birth is never shown to other users; we use it to mark your birthday;
  • your friends, friend requests you send or receive, and the people and groups you've blocked;
  • a secret code for your personal invite link, readable only by you. A friend request sent from your link carries it, so our relay can make you friends straight away; resetting the link replaces the code;
  • reports you send about a person or group: the reason, any details you add, and the last 5 messages from them in that chat (the report screen tells you this before you send). Only we can read reports, to review them; the person or group isn't told who reported them;
  • messages you send to friends, readable only by you and that friend, and when you last read each chat;
  • while you type in a chat, a short-lived "typing" marker that only that friend can see; it disappears a few seconds after you stop, send, leave the chat or go offline;
  • your online status and, if you share it, your current activity (above);
  • "ask to join" requests and room invites between friends;
  • crash reports: when BingeJam crashes or freezes, it sends a report so we can fix it. It contains the error, which screen was open, your phone model, Android and app version, your username, and BingeJam's own log lines from just before (with emails, links and tokens removed). It never contains your messages. Reports are encrypted on your phone so only our team can read them, and deleted after 7 days. This is on for everyone; we use it only to fix problems;
  • your phone model, Android version and BingeJam version, kept with your account;
  • a push token for each phone you're signed in on, so we can notify you about new messages and friend requests (removed when you sign out);
  • for watch parties and jams: room membership, the room's play/pause state and position, your periodically reported position (used for syncing), the host's now-playing title, length, artwork thumbnail and public track link (jams), room chat and reactions. A room's contents are readable only by its members; a room's preview can be read by any signed-in user who has the room code, so invitees can see what's playing before joining.

Why we use it (purpose and consent)

We process this data only to provide what you ask for: your account and profile, friends and messages, keeping playback in sync, showing friends the activity you chose to share, and notifications. You give consent when you create an account and when you grant each permission or turn on sharing for an app; you can withdraw it at any time by turning the feature off or deleting your account. We don't use your data for advertising, profiling or training, and we never sell or share it with anyone except the service providers below, who process it on our behalf.

Service providers

  • Google Firebase (Authentication, Realtime Database, Cloud Messaging, Hosting).
  • Cloudflare (the notification relay and profile-picture storage).
  • Watchmode (a film catalogue). When you join a friend's Netflix watch party, our relay sends Watchmode the movie's title and length (nothing about you) to find its Netflix link, so "Play in Netflix" opens that exact movie. The answer is cached on Cloudflare for up to 25 days.
  • GitHub (hosts app downloads; the app asks GitHub's public API once per launch whether a newer version exists. No personal data is sent; GitHub sees the request's IP address as with any web request).

When you message a friend or send a friend request, the app asks our relay to notify them. The relay checks it's really you, reads the message or request from our database and hands it to Firebase Cloud Messaging for delivery. It stores nothing. BingeJam contains no ads and no analytics SDKs, and the web pages use no trackers, ads, analytics or third-party scripts.

Optional permissions

  • Notifications: new messages and friend requests, and one quiet notification while you're in a watch party or jam so Android keeps syncing running. Turn either kind off in Android's notification settings.
  • Display over other apps: only if you turn on the floating chat bubble.
  • Battery optimisation exemption: only if you allow it, so syncing isn't stopped mid-movie.

How long we keep it

  • Account data, friends and messages: until you delete them or your account.
  • Activity: only while you're playing; cleared when you stop, go invisible or go offline.
  • Push tokens: until you sign out on that phone or delete your account.
  • Rooms: room state, chat and reactions are deleted when the last person leaves the room, or within an hour once nobody has been in it for 15 minutes. Until then they're readable only by its members (a room's preview by anyone with its code).
  • Reports: until we've reviewed them and dealt with any problem they describe.
  • Crash reports: 7 days.

Your rights

Under the DPDP Act you can:

  • access a summary of your data and how it's used (most of it is visible in the app; for the rest, email us);
  • correct or update it (edit your profile in the app, or email us);
  • erase it: Settings → Delete account in the app deletes your account and data immediately (how it works);
  • withdraw consent for any optional feature by turning it off;
  • nominate someone to exercise these rights if you die or become incapacitated (email us);
  • raise a grievance with our Grievance Officer (below). If you're not satisfied with our response, you may complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

Security

Data travels over encrypted connections (HTTPS/TLS). Database access rules ensure your private data is readable only by you, messages only by you and the friend, and activity only by friends. No system is perfectly secure; if a breach affecting your data occurs, we'll inform you and the Data Protection Board as the law requires.

Age

BingeJam is only for people aged 18 or older. We don't knowingly process data of anyone under 18; if you believe a child has an account, email us and we'll delete it.
